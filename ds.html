Nintendo DS

The Nintendo DS is a 32-bit foldable handheld game console produced by Nintendo. It was released in America in 2004. The DS introduced distinctive new features to handheld games: two Liquid Crystal Display (LCD) screens working in tandem (the bottom one being a touchscreen), a built-in microphone, and support for wireless connectivity. Prior to its release, the Nintendo DS was marketed as an experimental "third pillar" in Nintendo's console lineup, meant to complement the Game Boy Advance family and GameCube. However, backward compatibility (being able to use Game Boy Advance cartridges) and strong sales ultimately established it as the successor to the Game Boy series.
